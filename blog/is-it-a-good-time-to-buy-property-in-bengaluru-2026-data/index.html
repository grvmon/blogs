Is it a good time to buy property in Bengaluru? What the 2026 data says

a&k
acre&key
Acrenkey Realty LLP • 19 Oct 2026 • 11 min read
Share:

Short answer: Is it a good time to buy property in Bengaluru in 2026? If you'll live in it, it's a reasonable time, not a rush. Sales are rising slowly, unsold stock is up 35% in a year, prices grew 8% to 11%, and the RBI raised rates on 7 Oct 2026. Your own finances matter more than the market.

Key takeaways
  • Demand: steady. Q3 2026 sales rose 1% to 12% year on year depending on the tracker.
  • Supply: building. About 80,200 unsold homes, up 35% (ANAROCK), so you have choice.
  • Prices: still rising, 8% (ANAROCK) to 11% (Knight Frank) a year, but RBI's registration-based index shows a slower rise nationally (3.6%).
  • Rates: repo up to 5.50%; the RBI says cuts are off the table for now.
  • Rental yield: about 4% gross in the areas we checked, below home loan rates. Buy to live, not as a pure investment play.
  • Our test: if you'll hold 7+ years, have your cash-to-keys ready, and can carry the EMI at +2 pp, the market timing matters less than the property.

Everyone asking "is now a good time?" is really asking two questions: what's the market doing, and is it a good time for me? This page answers both with current data, and we update it every quarter.

Why do the price numbers differ?

The consultants quote base prices on new and unsold stock. Knight Frank says its 11% partly reflects more expensive stock entering the market. The RBI's House Price Index is built from registered transactions and covers 18 cities. Nationally it rose 3.6% in the year to Apr–Jun 2026. Bengaluru's index stood at 127.08 (provisional) against 117.49 for all-India, so Bengaluru registered prices have risen faster than the national average since 2022-23. We couldn't capture the city's year-on-year change from the RBI portal itself, so we don't quote one.

Are prices going to fall?

Nobody can tell you that honestly, and we won't guess. What the data does say:

  • Prices have kept rising through 2026 on every measure we checked.
  • Stock is building fast, which usually slows price growth before it reverses it. Knight Frank's Q3 view is that the market "is not overheating in aggregate".
  • Rates have turned up, which cools demand at the margin.

So the case for waiting for a big fall is weak on current evidence. The case for waiting because more stock gives you more choice and better terms is stronger.

Does buying beat renting right now?

Purely as an investment, not on rental income alone. Here's gross yield on ANAROCK's Q1 2026 quoted rents and base rates for a 1,000 sq ft 2BHK:

Area Rent per month (quoted range) Base rate per sq ft Gross yield (mid-rent ÷ base price)
Sarjapur Road ₹33,000–46,000 ₹11,400 about 4.2%
Whitefield ₹30,000–45,000 ₹10,680 about 4.2%
Electronic City ₹20,000–31,000 ₹7,200 about 4.3%

Source: ANAROCK Bengaluru Q1 2026. These are before maintenance, tax, vacancy and the cost of buying (stamp duty, registration, GST), so net yield is lower.

A 4% gross yield is about half a typical home loan rate. If you're buying with a loan to rent out, you're betting on price growth, not income. If you're buying to live in, the comparison is different: you're buying stability, control and an asset you'd otherwise rent.

What does the rate hike do to your EMI?

Worked example 1: two scenarios on a ₹75 lakh loan

Illustrative 20-year loan. Your bank's rate and when it passes changes through will differ.

Rate Monthly EMI vs 8.00%
8.00% ₹62,733
8.25% (one 0.25 pp hike passed on) ₹63,905 +₹1,172
8.50% (two hikes passed on) ₹65,087 +₹2,354

The RBI called its stance "calibrated tightening", meaning the next move is a hike or a pause. Plan for the 8.50% line, and stress-test at 2 points above your sanctioned rate. The home loan EMI calculator and home affordability calculator let you run your own numbers.

Set that against what waiting gives you. If another year lets you save ₹6 lakh more for the down payment, clear a loan, or find a better property from a bigger pool of unsold stock, waiting can still be the right call. If you're waiting only to "time the bottom", you're paying for a guess. Our first-time buyer guide has the full cost-of-waiting method.

The four-question timing test (our framework)

Market timing matters less than these four. If you can answer yes to all of them, it's a good time for you.

  1. Will you hold for 7 years or more? Buying costs (about 7.6% in government charges on resale, more with GST and extras) take years to earn back. The stamp duty calculator guide shows the exact figure.
  2. Is your cash-to-keys ready? Down payment, government charges, interiors and a buffer, in hand. Our total cost guide lists every line.
  3. Can you carry the EMI at +2 pp? If a 2-point rise would break your budget, the timing is wrong for you whatever the market does.
  4. Does the right property exist in your budget? With 52% of new launches above ₹1.5 crore, check this before anything else.

Then run the property-level checks: title, RERA, khata, water and flood. Start with our before-token checklist, then follow how to buy a flat in Bengaluru, step by step.

What mistakes do buyers make when timing the market?

  • Reading one headline number. Sales were +12% on one tracker and +1% on another in the same quarter.
  • Waiting for a crash with no plan. Every quarter of waiting has a cost if prices keep rising.
  • Rushing because of a "price hike from next month" pitch. With 80,200 unsold homes, you usually have time.
  • Buying to rent out on a loan at 4% yield. The maths relies on price growth.
  • Ignoring rates. The RBI's stance has changed; budget for higher EMIs.

What to do if...

...you're ready on all four questions? Shortlist with confidence. Use the stock build-up to negotiate on terms: payment plan, parking, extras.

...you fail question 3? Bring the loan down (bigger down payment, smaller home, co-borrower), or wait and save.

...your budget is under ₹80 lakh? Look at resale and ready flats, and check khata and water carefully.

...you're an investor? Compare the 4% gross yield with your loan rate and other options. Speak to a financial adviser.
