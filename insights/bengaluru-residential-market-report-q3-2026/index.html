Bengaluru real estate market 2026: Q3 residential report (July to September)

Nitin Gupta
Investments · Decision Framework • Published: 19 Oct 2026 • 12 min read
Share:

Short answer: The Bengaluru real estate market in 2026 grew slowly in Q3. Sales rose 1% to 12% year on year depending on the tracker, while ANAROCK counted 80,200 unsold homes, up 35% in a year. Average prices rose 8% (ANAROCK) to 11% (Knight Frank). Then on 7 Oct 2026, the RBI raised the repo rate to 5.50%.

Key takeaways
  • Sales: ANAROCK 16,650 (+12% YoY), Knight Frank 15,172 (+4%), PropEquity 17,340 (+1%). All three point up; the size of the rise depends on who counts.
  • Supply: launches of 17,700 (ANAROCK) to 18,393 (Knight Frank). Launches beat sales again.
  • Unsold stock: 80,200 homes (ANAROCK), up 35% YoY, the sharpest rise of the seven cities. Still the lowest overhang at 15 months.
  • Prices: ₹9,600 per sq ft (ANAROCK, +8%) and ₹9,794 (Knight Frank, +11%), measured differently.
  • Mix: 52% of new launches were priced above ₹1.5 crore; homes under ₹80 lakh were about 1%.
  • Rates: repo up 25 bps to 5.50% on 7 Oct 2026, and the RBI says rate cuts are off the table for now.

This is acre&key's quarterly read of the Bengaluru housing market. We put the main trackers side by side, show where they disagree and why, and translate the numbers into what they mean for a buyer this quarter. The raw figures are in a CSV at the end.

How many homes were sold in Bengaluru in Q3 2026?

Between 15,172 and 17,340, depending on the tracker.

Tracker Q3 2026 sales YoY QoQ What it counts
ANAROCK 16,650 +12% +9% Top 7 cities, own project survey
Knight Frank 15,172 +4% +2% 8 cities, own survey
PropEquity 17,340 +1% n/a 9 markets, as reported by Outlook Money

Sources: ANAROCK Q3 2026, Knight Frank Q3 2026, Outlook Money on PropEquity.

Why do they differ? Each firm builds its count from its own project survey, with different coverage and a different year-ago base. The absolute numbers sit within about 2,200 units of each other; the growth rates differ far more. Compare each tracker with its own history, never one tracker's quarter with another's year-ago number.

Bengaluru was India's second-largest housing market by volume in both ANAROCK's and Knight Frank's tables, behind Mumbai. In ANAROCK's data, only three of seven cities grew sales year on year: Hyderabad (+15%), Bengaluru (+12%) and MMR (+5%).

How much new supply came in, and how much is unsold?

Measure Q3 2026 Change Source
Launches (ANAROCK) 17,700 +16% YoY, −18% QoQ ANAROCK
Launches (Knight Frank) 18,393 +3% YoY Knight Frank
Available inventory 80,200 +35% YoY, +1% QoQ ANAROCK
Inventory overhang 15 months +22% YoY, −2% QoQ ANAROCK
Quarters to sell 5.5 up from 4.6, inventory +21% Knight Frank

ANAROCK says Bengaluru had the sharpest annual build-up of unsold stock among the seven cities. It also says the overhang is the lowest of the seven, and that it eased on the quarter because sales beat a smaller launch pipeline. Both are true: stock grew fast, but so did sales.

Worked example 1: what "15 months of overhang" means

Overhang is how long it would take to sell all unsold stock at the current sales pace.

Step Working Result
Monthly sales pace 16,650 ÷ 3 5,550 homes a month
Months to clear 80,200 80,200 ÷ 5,550 14.5 months (ANAROCK rounds to 15)
Knight Frank's version 5.5 quarters × 3 16.5 months
Q3 gap between launches and sales 17,700 − 16,650 1,050 more homes added than sold

The two firms land within two months of each other. If launches keep beating sales by about 1,000 homes a quarter, stock creeps up slowly rather than spiking.

What happened to property prices?

Tracker Average price YoY QoQ How it's measured
ANAROCK ₹9,600 per sq ft +8% +2% Base selling price on super built-up area
Knight Frank ₹9,794 per sq ft +11% +5% Weighted by unsold inventory in projects launched since 2019

Knight Frank itself says Bengaluru's 11% "is likely to reflect, in part, the rapid addition of higher-priced stock" to its inventory-weighted average. In plain terms, part of the rise is a change in what's on sale, not the same flat getting dearer. Outside NCR and Bengaluru, Knight Frank found annual price growth of 3% to 6%, close to inflation.

What's being built: who is the new supply for?

ANAROCK says 52% of Bengaluru's Q3 launches were priced above ₹1.5 crore, and affordable plus lower-mid homes (under ₹80 lakh) were about 1%. Bengaluru and Hyderabad were the most top-heavy markets on launch mix.

Knight Frank's national table points the same way: unsold stock in the ₹2–5 crore band rose 37% across its eight cities, with Bengaluru accounting for almost half of that increase.

If your budget is under ₹80 lakh, new launches in city limits are thin. Expect to look at resale, ready inventory, or the outer belt. If you're there, check water and flood risk carefully; our Cauvery Stage 5 guide and flood-prone areas guide cover both.

What does the RBI's October rate hike mean for buyers?

On 7 Oct 2026, the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% and changed its stance to "calibrated tightening". It said this signals that "rate cuts are off the table in the near term and policy action ahead can only be a rate hike or a pause". It projects CPI inflation of 6.0% for Oct–Dec 2026 (RBI, 7 Oct 2026). The next meeting is 2 to 4 Dec 2026.

Three-tracker read: our framework for any market report

Every quarter you'll see headlines quoting one number. Here's how we read them.

  1. Direction first. Do all trackers agree on direction? In Q3 2026, all three show sales up. That's a signal.
  2. Size second. If the size differs widely (1% vs 12%), treat the exact figure as soft and quote the range.
  3. Same tracker, same measure. Compare a firm only with its own history. ANAROCK's price is base selling price; Knight Frank's is inventory-weighted. They're not the same thing.
  4. Mix check. When averages jump, ask whether the mix of what's on sale changed.

Q3 2026 scorecard: sales up (3 of 3 agree), launches above sales (2 of 2), unsold stock up (2 of 2), prices up (2 of 2, with mix caveat), rates up (official).

What does Q3 2026 mean for you?

  • If you're buying to live in: demand is steady, stock is building and rates have turned up. That's a market where you can take your time and compare, not one where you need to rush. Our is it a good time to buy guide works through the decision.
  • If your budget is under ₹80 lakh: focus on resale and ready flats, and check the GBA-era khata changes that affect older properties.
  • If you're taking a large loan: budget for a higher rate.
  • If someone quotes "prices up 11%": ask which tracker, which measure and which area.
