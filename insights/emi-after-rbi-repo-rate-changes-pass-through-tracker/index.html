Your EMI after every RBI repo move: how much does each change pass through?

a&k
acre&key
Acrenkey Realty LLP • 19 Oct 2026 • 8 min read
Share:

Short answer: To work out the repo rate hike EMI impact, start from the RBI's 7 Oct 2026 move: repo up 25 bps to 5.50%. A repo-linked home loan must reset at least every three months, so expect it within one quarter. On an illustrative ₹80 lakh, 20-year loan, 25 bps adds about ₹1,260 a month to a new EMI.

Key takeaways
  • Five moves since early 2025: cuts to 6.25%, 6.00%, 5.50% and 5.25%, then the October 2026 hike back to 5.50%.
  • Each 25 bps is worth about ₹1,260 a month on a fresh ₹80 lakh, 20-year loan in this range.
  • Repo-linked loans reset at least once every three months. Your bank's reset date decides when you feel it.
  • You choose EMI or tenure. RBI rules require lenders to offer higher EMI, longer tenure, both, or prepayment.
  • Our Check–Choose–Cushion plan turns a rate letter into a decision.

What has the repo rate done since 2025?

Date Repo rate Change Illustrative loan rate (repo + 3.00%) EMI, ₹80 lakh over 20 years
Before Feb 2025 6.50% 9.50% ₹74,570
7 Feb 2025 6.25% −25 bps 9.25% ₹73,269
9 Apr 2025 6.00% −25 bps 9.00% ₹71,978
6 Jun 2025 5.50% −50 bps 8.50% ₹69,426
5 Dec 2025 5.25% −25 bps 8.25% ₹68,165
Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug 2026 5.25% Held 8.25% ₹68,165
7 Oct 2026 5.50% +25 bps 8.50% ₹69,426

The October 2026 decision raised repo to 5.50%, moved the stance to "calibrated tightening" and said "rate cuts are off the table in the near term" (RBI, 7 Oct 2026). The December 2025 cut to 5.25% is in the RBI's 5 Dec 2025 resolution. Earlier 2025 dates are from the RBI's policy statements.

The "repo + 3.00%" loan rate is illustrative. It gives 8.5% at today's repo, the rate we use across our guides. Your bank's spread depends on your credit profile and the lender. The next MPC meeting is 2–4 Dec 2026.

How fast does a repo change reach your EMI?

RBI rules require floating-rate retail loans, including home loans, to be linked to an external benchmark such as the repo rate. The rate under that benchmark must reset at least once every three months (RBI Master Direction, Interest Rate on Advances). So a repo-linked loan picks up the October hike at your next reset, at most about a quarter later. Loans on older MCLR or base-rate benchmarks move on their own schedule.

When a reset raises your EMI or tenure, the lender must tell you. You must be offered a higher EMI, a longer tenure, a mix of both, or part or full prepayment. Switching to a fixed rate is up to the lender's board-approved policy (RBI FAQs on floating-rate reset).

Worked example 1: the round trip since June 2025

Illustrative, a fresh ₹80 lakh, 20-year loan at repo + 3.00%:

Period Loan rate EMI Versus June 2025
Jun–Nov 2025 8.50% ₹69,426
Dec 2025–Oct 2026 8.25% ₹68,165 −₹1,261 a month
After Oct 2026 reset 8.50% ₹69,426 Back to where it was

The December cut and the October hike cancel out. Borrowers who took the 2025 cuts as lower EMIs are now back at June 2025's figure.

Worked example 2: raise the EMI or extend the tenure?

Illustrative. You owe ₹75 lakh with 216 months left at 8.25%, and the reset takes you to 8.50%.

Option What changes Cost over the loan
Raise the EMI ₹66,761 → ₹67,909 (+₹1,148 a month) About ₹2.48 lakh more in total
Keep the EMI, extend the tenure 216 → about 225 months (+9 months) About ₹6.03 lakh more in total

Extending feels painless but costs about two and a half times as much here. If your budget can take ₹1,148 a month, raising the EMI is the cheaper choice. Check that tenure extension doesn't push you past your lender's age limit.

The Check–Choose–Cushion plan (our framework)

  1. Check. Which benchmark is your loan on (repo/EBLR, MCLR or older)? What's your reset date, and your spread over repo?
  2. Choose. Higher EMI or longer tenure? Compare total cost with our EMI calculator. Ask whether a fixed-rate switch is offered, and at what fee.
  3. Cushion. Keep EMIs within a comfortable share of take-home (see how much loan your salary supports), and test a further 25–50 bps.

Checklist when a rate letter arrives

  • [ ] Benchmark named on the sanction letter (repo, T-bill, MCLR)
  • [ ] Reset date and new rate, in writing
  • [ ] Your spread over the benchmark, unchanged from the sanction letter
  • [ ] New EMI and new tenure for both options
  • [ ] Remaining tenure against your lender's age limit
  • [ ] Prepayment option and any charges, in writing from your lender
  • [ ] Whether a balance transfer to a lower spread is worth it
