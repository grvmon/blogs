Devanahalli real estate future: airport, aerospace park and zoning

Gaurav Mongia
Market Strategy · Product · Tech • Published: 19 Oct 2026 • 14 min read
Share:

Short answer: The Devanahalli real estate future rests on the airport, which is growing and adding capacity toward 80 million passengers a year by 2030. The other big driver weakened: Karnataka dropped the 1,777-acre Aerospace Park expansion in 2025 and asked for the land to stay farmland. Zoning, not headlines, decides which land benefits.

Key takeaways
  • The airport is real growth: 44.47 million passengers in FY 2025–26, up 6.2% (BIAL via The Hindu, news).
  • Capacity is being added: 51.5 million a year now, about 80 million by 2030 with Terminal 2 Phase 2 (Lok Sabha, July 2026).
  • Aerospace Park Phase II is off: acquisition of 1,777 acres in 13 villages was dropped; the Cabinet asked for a Permanent Special Agriculture Zone.
  • A second airport is years away: BIAL's 150 km exclusivity runs to the airport's 25th anniversary in 2033.
  • Zoning decides value: BIAAPA's master plan zone, conversion and layout approval matter more than distance to the airport.
  • Our RUNWAY check: records, use permitted, news vs notification, water, access, years to payoff.

Devanahalli gets sold as the next big thing almost every year. Some of it is grounded: the airport next door is one of India's busiest and still growing. Some of it isn't: plans get announced, renamed or dropped. This piece separates the drivers you can verify from the ones you can't, and shows how to test a plot or flat against them. For today's prices and water, see our Devanahalli area guide.

How big is the airport, and is it still growing?

Kempegowda International Airport handled 44.47 million passengers and 532,000 tonnes of cargo in FY 2025–26, according to BIAL. Traffic rose 6.2%, with international passengers up 23.9% to 7.23 million (The Hindu, 30 Apr 2026, news).

The government's figures on capacity are in a Lok Sabha reply of 23 July 2026 (USQ 774):

Measure Figure
Annual passenger capacity now 51.5 million
Daily capacity about 141,000
Average daily footfall, FY 2026–27 to June about 123,800
Terminal capacity expected by 2030 80 million a year
Terminal 2 after Phase 2 from 25 to 45 million a year

Worked example 1: how much headroom does the airport have?

Step Calculation Result
Daily use vs capacity 123,800 ÷ 141,000 87.8%
Annual use vs capacity 44.47 ÷ 51.5 million 86.3%
Years to fill 51.5 million at 6.2% growth ln(51.5 ÷ 44.47) ÷ ln(1.062) about 2.4 years
Years to fill 80 million at 6.2% growth ln(80 ÷ 44.47) ÷ ln(1.062) about 9.8 years

At last year's growth rate, today's terminals would be full in under three years, which is why the 2030 expansion matters. If it lands on time, there's room for growth well into the next decade. That's a steady case for airport-linked jobs and rentals nearby. It isn't a case for every plot within 20 km. The growth rate here is last year's, applied flatly, as an illustration.

What happened to the Aerospace Park expansion?

This is the change most marketing hasn't caught up with.

  • The plan: KIADB began acquiring 1,777 acres in 13 villages of Channarayapatna hobli, Devanahalli taluk, for Aerospace Park Phase II. The preliminary notification was issued on 27 August 2021. Final notifications on 28 June 2024 and 19 April 2025 covered 1,281 acres in 10 villages (Indian Express, news).
  • The protest: farmers protested for 1,198 days (The Hindu, news).
  • The reversal: the Chief Minister announced on 15 July 2025 that the acquisition would be dropped. The Cabinet approved it on 4 December 2025, and a government order followed on 8 December. Farmers who want to can still sell to KIADB by consent (Indian Express, news).
  • The zoning change: the Cabinet note asked the Urban Development Department (BIAAPA) to notify the area as a "Permanent Special Agriculture Zone" in its master plan, to keep it green.

We didn't find the master plan amendment itself, so we can't confirm it has been notified. If you're looking at land in those 13 villages, check the zone with BIAAPA directly.

The first phase of the Aerospace Park, already developed, isn't affected.

What does this mean for buyers? Any pitch built on "the aerospace expansion next door" needs rechecking. Land in those villages was meant to stay agricultural, so a housing layout there would face a zoning problem, not a boom. Land outside them isn't directly affected, but one big demand driver that brochures counted on is off the table for now.

How does zoning decide what you can build?

Devanahalli's planning authority is BIAAPA, the Bengaluru International Airport Area Planning Authority, one of the local planning authorities listed by BMRDA. Its master plan zones land for residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural and public use.

Question Why it matters What to see
What zone is the survey number in? Housing can't be approved in an agricultural or green zone BIAAPA zoning for the survey number
Has the land been converted? Farmland needs conversion before it can be built on Conversion order (our guide)
Is the layout or building approved? Without approval, there's no valid site or plan BIAAPA layout or building approval
Are sites released? Unreleased sites can't be sold as approved Release letter
Any acquisition or special zone? Notified land can be frozen or taken Check against KIADB and master plan notifications

Will there be a second airport, and does it matter for Devanahalli?

Not soon. The Airports Authority of India did pre-feasibility studies of three sites for Karnataka, but the Centre hasn't received a proposal under the Greenfield Airports Policy (USQ 774). BIAL's concession bars any new airport within 150 km before the 25th anniversary of its opening (concession agreement, s.5.2), which the state minister put at 2033 (The Hindu, Dec 2025, news). A second airport would share growth eventually, but the site and timing are unknown.

How will you get there?

  • Metro: the Blue Line's Hebbal–airport stretch is targeted for September 2027 (The Hindu, news). It serves the airport, not Devanahalli town (station names per news reports).
  • Suburban rail: Corridor-1 runs from KSR Bengaluru to Devanahalli, with land acquisition under way and a reported March 2030 target. See our suburban rail stations guide.

Worked example 2: the holding cost of a speculative plot

You buy a ₹30 lakh plot near Devanahalli to hold for five years, expecting the area to "take off". It earns nothing meanwhile. Compare it with a deposit at an illustrative 7% a year.

Year Deposit value at 7%
Today ₹30,00,000
After 5 years ₹42,07,655

The plot has to sell for about ₹42 lakh, a 40% rise, just to match the deposit, before brokerage, charges and the cost of fencing or upkeep. And if the plot's zone, conversion or approval is wrong, you may not be able to sell at all. Speculative plots in this belt depend on the paperwork more than the airport. The RUNWAY check (our framework)

  • R: Records. Title, RTC and khata in the seller's name, with no acquisition notice.
  • U: Use permitted. The BIAAPA zone for the survey number, and whether a special agriculture zone applies.
  • N: News vs notification. Count a project only once it's notified or under construction. Aerospace Park Phase II was notified and still dropped.
  • W: Water. Devanahalli isn't on the Cauvery network; check the project's own source.
  • A: Access. The metro reaches the airport, not the town. Suburban rail is years off.
  • Y: Years to payoff. Work out the holding cost against a safe return before you assume growth.

Checklist before you buy in Devanahalli

  • [ ] BIAAPA zone confirmed for the survey number
  • [ ] Not in the 13 Channarayapatna hobli villages, or zone status confirmed with BIAAPA if it is
  • [ ] Conversion order seen
  • [ ] Layout or building approval from BIAAPA seen
  • [ ] Sites released, for a plotted layout

What mistakes do buyers make?

  • Buying on announcements. The Aerospace Park expansion was notified and still dropped.
  • Ignoring zoning. Agricultural or green zone land can't be approved for housing.
  • Counting the airport metro as town metro.
  • Assuming a second airport won't affect growth. It's not before 2033, but it is coming eventually.
  • Skipping conversion and release checks on plotted layouts.
  • Forgetting holding costs on land that earns nothing.

What should you do if...

...a seller says land is near the "aerospace SEZ expansion"? Ask which village. If it's one of the 13 villages where acquisition was dropped, check whether the special agriculture zone has been notified.

...the plot is in a green or agricultural zone? Don't buy it as a housing plot. Conversion isn't likely to be approved there.

...you work at the airport? Devanahalli and nearby areas can make sense on commute alone. Buy for that, not for a forecast.

...you're comparing with Yelahanka? See our Devanahalli vs Yelahanka comparison.

...the layout says "BMRDA approved"? In the airport area, the planning authority is BIAAPA. Check who signed the approval. Our approvals guide explains the difference.
