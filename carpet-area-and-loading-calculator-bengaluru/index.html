Carpet area and loading calculator for Bengaluru flat buyers

Nitin Gupta Co-founder
Last modified: 22 Oct 2026 • 7 min read
Share:

Short answer: Loading is the super built-up area minus the RERA carpet area, divided by the carpet area. A flat quoted at 1,350 sq ft with a 1,000 sq ft carpet area has 35% loading, so you pay 1.35 times the quoted rate for each square foot you can use. RERA sets no loading limit. It defines carpet area and makes builders disclose it.

Key takeaways
  • Carpet area is the legal measure: section 2(k) of the RERA Act counts the usable floor inside the flat plus internal walls.
  • Balconies and terraces are separate: exclusive balconies, verandahs and open terraces are left out of carpet area and listed on their own.
  • Super built-up is a sales figure: the Act does not define it and sets no loading limit.
  • Loading = (super built-up − carpet) ÷ carpet. Price per sq ft of carpet = quoted rate × super built-up ÷ carpet.
  • Check three documents: the RERA project page, the agreement for sale and the cost sheet should show the same carpet area.

What the law counts as carpet area

Section 2(k) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 defines carpet area as "the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding the area covered by the external walls, areas under services shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah area and exclusive open terrace area, but includes the area covered by the internal partition walls of the apartment".

"Super built-up area" is not defined in the Act. It is a sales figure. It usually adds walls, balconies and a share of common areas such as lobbies, staircases and amenities to the carpet area. Section 2(n) of the Act lists what counts as common areas.

What the calculator works out

  • Loading on carpet area = (super built-up − carpet) ÷ carpet × 100. This is how much the quoted area is "loaded" above the carpet area.
  • Carpet share = carpet ÷ super built-up × 100. Some people call this efficiency. It is the same comparison seen the other way.
  • Everything else = super built-up − carpet − exclusive balcony − exclusive terrace. That is the external walls, shafts and the common-area share, together.
  • Price per unit of carpet area = rate × super built-up ÷ carpet. Use the rate on your own cost sheet.
  • Units: 1 square metre = 10.7639 square feet. RERA project pages often give areas in square metres.

Where to find the real numbers

The RERA project page

Section 4(2)(h) of the Act requires the promoter to disclose the number, type and carpet area of the apartments for sale, with exclusive balcony, verandah and open terrace areas shown separately. Look the project up on the Karnataka RERA website. Our guide on checking a project on RERA Karnataka shows where.

The agreement for sale

Under section 13(2), the agreement for sale must set out the particulars of the development, including the construction of the apartment and its specifications. Check that the carpet area there matches the RERA page and the unit you were shown.

The brochure and the cost sheet

Ask the sales team to write the carpet area next to every super built-up figure they quote. Section 12 makes the promoter liable for loss caused by a false statement in an advertisement or prospectus, so keep copies.

At possession

Measure room by room, wall to wall, inside the external walls, and add internal partition walls. If the total is clearly short of the agreement, see our answer on what to do when the flat is smaller than the agreement says.

Verify with your lawyer: this page explains the RERA Act's definitions and does arithmetic. It is not legal advice. Before you sign, have your own property lawyer check how the agreement for sale states the carpet area and what it says about any shortfall.
