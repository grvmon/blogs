25 final checks before taking possession of your flat

G
By Gaurav • 19 Oct 2026 • 8 min read
Share:

Short answer: The final checks before taking possession are the OC naming your tower, a carpet area that matches the agreement, a written snag list, a final demand that matches your payment plan, and a possession letter that states the handover date. That date starts your five-year RERA defect window, so it must be in writing.

Key takeaways
  • OC first: a possession letter isn't an occupancy certificate.
  • Measure, then sign: check carpet area against the agreement before you accept the flat.
  • Snags in writing: list them, date them, and don't sign a letter that says you accept the flat's condition.
  • Your clock: RERA gives you five years from handover to report defects, and the builder 30 days to fix them.
  • Our SNAG check: statutory papers, nuts and bolts, accounts, get it in writing.

Handover day feels like the end. Legally, it starts two clocks: your defect window, and your share of maintenance and taxes. Here's what to check in the week before you take the keys in Bengaluru.

S: Are the statutory papers in hand?

  1. The OC for your tower. Check the issuer, the date and that your block is named. See our OC and CC guide.
  2. The completion certificate, where the local authority issues one.
  3. RERA status. The project page should show completion filings. Our RERA lookup guide shows where.
  4. Possession letter with the handover date written in.
  5. Sale deed registered, or a date fixed. Use our registration checklist.
  6. Khata plan. Ask when the unit-wise e-Khata will be ready, then plan the mutation.
  7. Plans, including common areas. Section 19(5) of the RERA Act entitles you to them after physical possession (RERA Act).

N: Do the nuts and bolts work?

  1. Carpet area measured against the agreement. Carpet area under s.2(k) is the net usable floor area inside your external walls; our carpet area explainer has the details.
  2. Walls and ceilings: cracks, damp patches, uneven paint.
  3. Floors: tap tiles for hollow sounds; check the level.
  4. Doors and windows: open, close and lock each one; check the seals.
  5. Bathrooms and balconies: pour water and watch it drain away from the room.
  6. Plumbing: run every tap and flush; look under sinks.
  7. Electricals: test every switch and socket; check the board is labelled.
  8. Fittings counted against the specification list in your agreement.
  9. Common areas: lifts, fire equipment, your allotted parking slot in writing.

A: Do the accounts add up?

  1. Final demand matches the payment plan in your agreement, line by line.
  2. Receipts for every payment, in the names on the agreement.
  3. Maintenance advance and corpus, with the rate, period and holder written down.
  4. Utility meters and deposits: who paid, and when they move into your name. See our BESCOM and BWSSB page.
  5. TDS on the final instalment, if the total price is ₹50 lakh or more. Ask your CA.

G: Have you got it in writing?

  1. Snag list given to the builder, dated and signed, with fix dates.
  2. Possession letter wording. Don't sign a line saying you accept the condition of the flat while snags are open.
  3. Warranty cards for lifts, pumps, geysers and fittings.
  4. Association and common areas. RERA requires allottees to take part in forming an association (s.19(9)) and the promoter to convey common areas to it (s.17).

Worked examples

Worked example 1: a carpet area shortfall

Illustrative figures. Your agreement says 1,000 sq ft carpet at ₹9,000 per sq ft of carpet, so ₹90,00,000. Your measurements come to 975 sq ft.

Item Figure
Agreement carpet area 1,000 sq ft
Your measurement 975 sq ft
Gap 25 sq ft (2.5%)
Value of gap at ₹9,000 ₹2,25,000

Your tape isn't an official survey, so a few square feet means little. A 2.5% gap is worth raising. Ask for the builder's measurement sheet, and check what your agreement says about variations before you sign. Your lawyer can advise on the clause.

Worked example 2: the dates and money on handover day

Illustrative figures. The OC is issued on 15 Jan 2027, and you take possession on 1 Mar 2027.

Item Working Result
Possession deadline, s.19(10) 2 months from OC 15 Mar 2027
Defect window, s.14(3) 5 years from handover to about 1 Mar 2032
Builder's fix time per defect notice 30 days
Final instalment 5% of ₹90,00,000 ₹4,50,000
TDS on it 1% ₹4,500
Maintenance advance ₹4/sq ft × 1,350 sq ft × 12 months (assumed rate) ₹64,800

Ask for your project's actual maintenance rate; ₹4 is an assumption. The defect window runs from the handover date on your letter, which is why that date matters (RERA Act). If the builder was late, our delayed possession guide covers your options.

The SNAG check (our framework)

Letter What it covers Checks
S: Statutory papers OC, CC, RERA status, deed, khata, plans 1 to 7
N: Nuts and bolts Area, finishes, plumbing, electricals, common areas 8 to 16
A: Accounts Final demand, receipts, deposits, TDS 17 to 21
G: Get it in writing Snag list, letter wording, warranties, association 22 to 25

If any S item is missing, pause. N items go on the snag list. A items need receipts. G items need signatures and dates.

What to carry on inspection day

  • [ ] Agreement for sale, floor plan and specification list
  • [ ] Measuring tape and a torch
  • [ ] Phone charger to test sockets
  • [ ] A bottle of water for slope tests
  • [ ] A notepad, or a phone for numbered photos
  • [ ] Your payment receipts

After the keys

Move the khata and utilities into your name, keep your snag list until every item is closed, and set a reminder before your defect window ends. Our first 90 days guide covers the order.

Lawyer note

The possession letter, carpet area clauses and defect claims turn on your agreement. Have a property lawyer read the letter before you sign it. Ask a CA about TDS.
