Devanahalli is the old fort town next to Kempegowda International Airport, about where the city's northern growth meets farmland. Many people look here because of the airport and the aerospace and IT parks planned around it. This guide sticks to what official records and named sources say, and flags what nobody has published.

Who governs Devanahalli?

Three bodies matter, and none is the Greater Bengaluru Authority:

  1. Devanahalli Town Municipal Council runs the town, its property records and water supply (Bengaluru Rural district site).
  2. Gram panchayats run villages outside the town. Their property records sit on e-Swathu.
  3. BIAAPA, the Bengaluru International Airport Area Planning Authority, is the local planning authority for the Devanahalli area, one of the authorities listed by BMRDA under the KTCP Act (BMRDA).

This affects what you pay and what you check. The stamp duty surcharge is 2% of duty in municipal areas and 3% in panchayat areas. A layout should have land conversion, BIAAPA approval and released sites. Our plot buyer's guide and approvals guide walk through these checks.

Market prices

This is the honest gap. Knight Frank's Bengaluru price table doesn't include Devanahalli, and ANAROCK's market tables cover other areas. Listing portals publish asking prices, which aren't evidence of what anything sold for. We have no official or consultant market series for Devanahalli to cite. The most useful evidence is registered values for the same layout or building, from encumbrance certificates on Kaveri.

What we can say: Knight Frank calls North Bengaluru the city's fastest-growing residential corridor, with launches up 10% and sales up 11% in H1 2026.

What's being built

Devanahalli taluk wasn't among the three east taluks in our RERA study, so we don't have a delivery figure. Check each project's completion date and extensions on Karnataka RERA. Plotted layouts must be RERA-registered too, above the size threshold.

Is there a metro to Devanahalli?

Not to the town. The Blue Line runs from Central Silk Board via KR Puram and Hebbal to the airport, with stations at Airport City and the terminals (station names per news reports). The Hebbal–airport section is targeted for September 2027, and the full line for March 2028, according to the Bengaluru development minister (Times of India, 9 Oct 2026, news). Deccan Herald reported in August 2026 that work beyond Yelahanka was lagging, so treat these as targets.

From the town, you'd still need a road trip to the nearest airport-line station. Time it before you count it.

Where does Devanahalli get its water?

Devanahalli isn't part of Bengaluru's Cauvery network. The town council has relied largely on groundwater. Since August 2024, a shallow-aquifer treatment plant near Sihineerukere has supplied about 6.4 lakh litres a day, roughly 10% of the town's demand. The lake is recharged partly with treated wastewater from the Hebbal–Nagawara valley (The Hindu, Oct 2024, news).

A ₹44.87 crore water supply scheme for the town is listed under AMRUT 2.0 at "DPR prepared" stage (AMRUT 2.0 platform). That isn't a completion date. In villages and new layouts, expect borewells and tankers unless a project proves otherwise. Our water questions list what to ask.

Is Devanahalli flood-prone?

There's no public flood-point list for Devanahalli; the city's list covers only the five Bengaluru corporations. The risk to check is site-level: tank beds, valley drains and low plots in new layouts. Lake buffers became size-based (0 to 30 m) under Act 19 of 2026, measured from the lake's revenue boundary. Our lake and drain checks show how to pull the survey maps, and a visit after heavy rain tells you the rest.

Devanahalli buyer's checklist

  • [ ] Town council or panchayat confirmed from the tax receipt
  • [ ] Property record (e-Aasthi or e-Swathu Form 9/11) matches the deed
  • [ ] Land conversion order and BIAAPA layout or building approval seen
  • [ ] Site released by the planning authority, if it's in a layout
  • [ ] Guidance value line found on Kaveri for the exact property
  • [ ] Past registered values checked on encumbrance certificates
  • [ ] Water source confirmed in writing
  • [ ] Tank beds and drains checked on survey maps; site seen after heavy rain
  • [ ] RERA registration checked for projects and larger layouts
  • [ ] Title checked by a lawyer (our legal checks guide)

What mistakes do buyers make in Devanahalli?

  • Treating "near the airport" as a price. There's no published market series; use registered values.
  • Counting the airport metro as a town metro. It serves the airport.
  • Skipping the approval chain. Conversion, BIAAPA approval and release are separate papers.
  • Assuming piped water. The town's own scheme is still at the project-report stage.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Ask a property lawyer to check title, conversion and approvals, and a CA about the tax side.
