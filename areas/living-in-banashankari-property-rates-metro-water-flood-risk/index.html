Banashankari is a large BDA-planned area in south-west Bengaluru, laid out in six stages from Kanakapura Road out towards the ring roads. The inner stages are older and built up; the 6th Stage is newer and more spread out. This guide uses official records and named sources, and says where data runs out.

Who governs Banashankari?

Two corporations. All of Banashankari 2nd Stage's PIN 560070 is in the South corporation. PINs 560050 and 560085, covering Kathriguppe, Srinivasa Nagara and nearby wards, are all in the West corporation. All are inside GBA, so stamp duty, cess, surcharge and registration add up to 7.6%. Check your plot with our ward and corporation lookup.

What are property rates in Banashankari?

Market prices

Knight Frank's H1 2026 report and ANAROCK's 2026 reports don't list Banashankari. The nearest Knight Frank benchmark is Kanakapura Road, at ₹5,300 to ₹10,100 per sq ft, up 6% in a year (Knight Frank). Treat that as context only: the inner stages are older and more central. Portal averages are listing prices, not registrations.

Is there a metro in Banashankari?

Yes. The Green Line runs along Kanakapura Road on Banashankari's eastern side. Its extension from Yelachenahalli to Silk Institute opened on 14 January 2021 (PIB).

The western stages, especially 6th Stage, are a long way from the line. Our metro lines guide covers planned lines too.

Where does Banashankari get its water?

The inner stages are older BDA layouts with long-standing BWSSB connections. The outer stages and nearby villages are more mixed, and some depend on borewells and tankers. The Bengaluru South and RR Nagar zones are both covered by Cauvery Stage 5, which by May 2026 had connected about 1.15 lakh of an estimated 1.8 lakh buildings in the 2007 villages (The Hindu, news). Ask for the BWSSB connection and recent bills; our Cauvery Stage 5 guide explains how.

Is Banashankari flood-prone?

We didn't find a 2026 official list naming Banashankari locations. In April 2026, the South City Corporation had 24 of the city's 101 listed flood-prone locations and the West had 48 (The New Indian Express, news). Those figures cover each whole corporation, not this area.

Low streets, cellars and plots near old tank beds are the ones to check. See our lake and drain checks and flood-prone areas map.

Banashankari buyer's checklist

  • [ ] Confirm the ward, corporation and khata.
  • [ ] Find the exact stage, block and site row on Kaveri.
  • [ ] Work out the price-to-guidance ratio.
  • [ ] Check the BDA allotment and absolute sale deed.
  • [ ] Check the plan sanction and OC.
  • [ ] Visit after heavy rain.
  • [ ] Time the trip to the nearest Green Line station.
  • [ ] Ask for BWSSB bills and tanker spend.

What mistakes do buyers make in Banashankari?

  • Treating it as one market. Guidance values vary four-fold between stages.
  • Using portal averages as prices. They're listings, not registrations.
  • Skipping the BDA paper trail. Allotment, possession and absolute sale deed all matter.
  • Assuming the metro is close. The western stages are far from the line.
