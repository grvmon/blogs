Hennur vs Thanisandra: the data side by side

N
By Nitin • 19 Oct 2026 • 10 min read
Share:

Short answer: Hennur vs Thanisandra is a close call on price. Knight Frank puts Hennur at ₹7,000–14,000 per sq ft (+10% in a year) and Thanisandra at ₹5,700–13,500 (+6%). Neither has a running metro; the Blue Line's KR Puram–Hebbal stretch is expected by December 2027 to March 2028. Only Thanisandra has a published rent figure.

Key takeaways
  • Price: at the midpoints, a 1,000 sq ft flat costs ₹9 lakh more in Hennur (Knight Frank H1 2026).
  • Rent: ANAROCK puts Thanisandra Main Road 2BHK rents at ₹28,000–42,000 a month; it publishes none for Hennur.
  • Metro: both wait for the Blue Line (news). Hennur is also a planned station on suburban Corridor-4.
  • Floors: main-road guidance values are almost the same, ₹78,000 and ₹80,000 per sq m.
  • Our PATH test: pocket, arterial, transit, holding cost.

Hennur and Thanisandra sit next to each other north of the outer ring road, both inside GBA, both feeding Manyata Tech Park. This page puts our Hennur guide and Thanisandra guide side by side.

Is Hennur or Thanisandra cheaper?

Thanisandra, slightly. Its range starts ₹1,300 per sq ft lower and ends ₹500 lower. Hennur grew faster over the last year, +10% against +6% (Knight Frank H1 2026).

Official floors overlap. Hennur village is ₹50,000 per sq m and the Hennur–Bagaluru main road ₹80,000. Thanisandra Main Road is ₹78,000, with Hegde Nagar behind it at ₹33,000.

Which has better metro access?

Neither has a running station. The Blue Line's KR Puram–Hebbal stretch, with stations including HBR Layout, Kalyan Nagar and Nagawara (station names per news reports), is expected by December 2027 to March 2028 (Times of India, 9 Oct 2026, news). Nagawara will also be the Pink Line's northern end, but that underground stretch has no firm date.

Hennur adds a planned suburban rail station on Corridor-4 (K-RIDE Samparka, May 2026), with no revised completion date. Our metro lines guide tracks both.

Which has better water supply?

They're in the same position. Both include villages added to the city in 2007 and covered by Cauvery Stage 5. By May 2026, about 1.15 lakh of an estimated 1.8 lakh buildings in those villages were connected (The Hindu, news). Ask for the BWSSB connection and bills; our Cauvery Stage 5 guide shows how.

Which is more flood-prone?

Hennur's side has the fresher record. Sai Layout, next to Hennur, flooded again on 18 May 2026, along with homes beside drains in Kalyan Nagar and Horamavu (The New Indian Express, news). In Thanisandra, low pockets near Rachenahalli lake and the valley drains need checking. Use our lake and drain checks for the site.

Worked example 2: the rent Hennur would need

Thanisandra's gross yield, using ANAROCK's figures: a 1,000 sq ft flat at ₹10,100 is ₹1,01,00,000. The midpoint rent of ₹35,000 a month gives ₹4,20,000 a year, or about 4.2%.

To match that yield at Hennur's midpoint price of ₹1,05,00,000, a flat would need about ₹36,386 a month. There's no published Hennur rent to test that against, so ask for recent rental agreements in the building. Our rent vs buy page covers the other side of the decision.
