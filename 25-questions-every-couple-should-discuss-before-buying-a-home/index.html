25 questions for couples before buying a home

Gaurav Mongia
Market Strategy · Product · Tech • Published: 19 Oct 2026 • 8 min read
Share:

Short answer: The key questions for couples before buying a home are whose names go on the deed and in what shares, who pays the down payment and EMI, whether you can manage on one income, which tax regime each of you uses, and what happens if one of you dies, stops working or you separate. Agree these in writing before booking.

Key takeaways
  • Shares should match money: write each person's share in the sale deed, and keep a record of who paid what.
  • Plan for one income: a joint loan sized on two salaries can take over half of one salary.
  • Tax benefits are per person: each co-owner who also repays the loan claims separately, in the old regime only for a self-occupied home.
  • No stamp duty discount for women in Karnataka, so adding a name doesn't cut the 7.6%.
  • Our SPLIT test: shares, payments, loan, insurance, tomorrow.

Couples usually agree on the flat long before they've agreed on the money. These questions are for the two of you, at a table, before the first site visit. Once you're aligned on budget, evaluate prospective locations using our neighbourhood checklist for families. For general pre-search questions, see our 25 questions before house hunting.

Whose home is it, on paper?

  1. Will the home be in one name or both?
  2. In what shares: 50:50, or in proportion to what each of you pays?
  3. Will the shares be written in the sale deed?
  4. Are there any existing claims on either of you, such as family property or guarantees?
  5. Does either of you plan to gift a share to the other later? Between spouses inside the GBA, that carries ₹5,000 stamp duty plus cess and surcharge, not the sale rate (our women buyers page).

More on this topic sits under Buyer Checklists & Process.

Who pays for what?

  1. How will you split the down payment?
  2. Who pays the roughly 7.6% stamp duty and registration?
  3. How will you split the EMI, and from whose account?
  4. Will you keep a shared record of every payment?
  5. Who pays maintenance, property tax and repairs?

A related buyer note is 20 questions to ask before.

Can you afford it on one income?

  1. If one of you stops working for a year, can the other pay the EMI?
  2. Is the loan sized on both incomes, or on the higher one?
  3. Do either of you have other loans a lender will count?
  4. How big an emergency fund will you keep after the down payment?
  5. Will children, a career break or a move change this in five years?

A related buyer note is Before we recommend a flat.

How will tax and the loan work?

  1. Will you both be co-borrowers and co-owners?
  2. Which tax regime does each of you use? Interest on a self-occupied home only helps in the old regime.
  3. Have you asked a CA how to split shares for tax?
  4. Whose credit record is stronger, and does that change the rate offered?

See our joint home loan rules page and home loan tax page.

What if things change?

  1. Do you each have term insurance at least equal to your share of the loan?
  2. Have you each made a will, and named nominees?
  3. If you separate, who keeps the home, and how is the other paid out?
  4. If one of you dies, can the other keep paying the EMI?
  5. Would you sell or rent it out if you had to move cities?
  6. What would make either of you walk away from a flat you both like?

Worked examples

These use the approved 8.5% illustrative rate over 20 years.

Worked example 1: do 50:50 shares match what you paid?

A ₹1 crore flat. The loan is ₹75 lakh (75% loan-to-value), so cash needed is ₹25 lakh down plus ₹7.6 lakh in charges, ₹32.6 lakh in all. Partner A puts in ₹22.6 lakh, Partner B ₹10 lakh. The EMI of ₹65,087 is split 40:60.

After 5 years Partner A Partner B
Upfront cash ₹22,60,000 ₹10,00,000
EMI share for 60 months ₹26,035 × 60 = ₹15,62,082 ₹39,052 × 60 = ₹23,43,123
Total paid ₹38,22,082 ₹33,43,123
Share of total paid 53% 47%

Close to 50:50 after five years, but not on day one, when A had paid 69% of the cash. Agree the shares knowing how the balance moves, and write them in the deed.

Worked example 2: the one-income test

Partner A takes home ₹1,20,000 a month and Partner B ₹80,000.

Both incomes A's income alone
EMI at 35% of take-home ₹70,000 ₹42,000
Loan that EMI supports about ₹80.66 lakh about ₹48.40 lakh
The ₹65,087 EMI as a share of income 33% 54%

If B takes a year off, the EMI takes 54% of A's pay. That's past the 50% line banks rarely lend beyond (our affordability study). Plan a buffer, or a smaller loan.

The SPLIT test (our framework)

Letter What you agree Written where
S: Shares Names and percentages Sale deed
P: Payments Who pays down payment, charges, EMI, upkeep Shared payment record
L: Loan Co-borrowers, sized to survive on one income Loan sanction
I: Insurance Term cover for each person's share of the loan Policies, nominees
T: Tomorrow Wills, separation, death, moving cities Wills, a written agreement

Couple's checklist

  • [ ] Agree names and shares, and put them in the sale deed.
  • [ ] Record every payment from each account.
  • [ ] Test the EMI against the higher single income.
  • [ ] Ask a CA how to set shares for tax, for each regime.
  • [ ] Buy term cover for each person's loan share.
  • [ ] Make wills and update nominees.
  • [ ] Discuss what happens if you separate.
  • [ ] Run the numbers on charges with our stamp duty calculator.

CA note

How shares, EMIs and regimes affect each partner's tax is specific to your incomes. Ask a CA before you fix the shares.

Lawyer note

Ownership shares, gifts between spouses, wills and what happens on separation are legal questions. Ask a property lawyer, and see our gift deed page and legal checks guide.
