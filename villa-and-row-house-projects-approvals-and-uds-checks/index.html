Villa and row-house projects in Bengaluru: approvals and UDS checks

a&k
acre&key
Acrenkey Realty LLP • 19 Oct 2026 • 9 min read
Share:

Short answer: Villa project approval checks start with one question: does the deed give you a marked plot, or an undivided share (UDS) of the whole site? That decides what you can rebuild, sell and borrow against. Then check the land conversion, layout approval, building plan sanction, RERA registration and an occupancy certificate for each villa.

Key takeaways
  • Plot or share: some villa projects sell a marked plot with the house; others are structured like apartments, with an undivided share of the site.
  • A bigger UDS isn't more usable land: in our example, a 2,178 sq ft UDS is a share of everything, while a 1,198 sq ft plot is land you can stand on and fence.
  • RERA counts villas as apartments: the Act's definition covers any self-contained unit "by any other name". The exemption is 500 sq m of land or 8 units.
  • Approvals stack: conversion, layout, building plan, commencement, then an occupancy certificate.
  • Our DEED test: division, entitlement, exit, dues.

A villa feels like owning a house, but the paperwork can look like owning a flat. This guide covers what to ask for and how to read it. It builds on our plot approval checks and building approval guide.

What are you actually buying in a villa project?

One of two things, and the sale deed tells you which:

Marked plot model Undivided share (UDS) model
What the deed conveys A plot with fixed boundaries, plus the house on it The villa, plus an undivided share in the land and common areas
Roads and parks Often handed to the local authority, or held in common Held in common by all owners
Rebuilding or extending Within the plot and sanctioned plan Usually needs the other owners and the plan
Selling the land alone Possible, as a plot Not really; the share moves with the villa
Owners' body Society or association Possibly under the Apartment Ownership Act, if the project qualifies

Neither is automatically better. Each is a different thing to own, so price it on that basis.

Do villa projects need RERA registration?

Usually. The RERA Act defines an "apartment" as a separate, self-contained part of a property, "whether called block, chamber, dwelling unit, flat... or by any other name", in a building or on a plot of land. A villa fits that. A project is exempt only where the land doesn't exceed 500 sq m or there are no more than eight units, counting all phases (Section 3(2)(a)).

The developer must convey each unit with an undivided proportionate title in the common areas to the association (Sections 11 and 17). Check the project on Karnataka RERA and download the uploaded approvals. Our RERA project review guide shows how.

Which approvals should a villa project have?

In order:

  1. Title and any joint development agreement between landowner and developer.
  2. Land conversion from agricultural to residential use, if the land was agricultural.
  3. Layout or development plan approval from the authority with jurisdiction: BDA, a local planning authority under BMRDA, or GBA.
  4. Building plan sanction for the villa types.
  5. Commencement certificate before work starts, where the authority issues one.
  6. Occupancy certificate for the completed villas. See OC and CC explained.

A brochure saying "BMRDA approved" isn't an approval. Ask for the number, the date and the authority named on the document, then check it with that authority.

Does the Apartment Ownership Act apply to villas?

Sometimes. The Karnataka Apartment Ownership Act 1972 applies only where the owners submit the property to it by a registered Declaration, and the property is mainly residential (Section 2). It defines a "building" as one with four or more apartments, or two or more buildings each with two or more apartments, totalling four or more (Section 3(e)).

Row houses and semi-detached pairs read closer to that definition than detached villas do. Ask the developer which law the owners' association will be formed under, and have a lawyer confirm it from the sanctioned plan.

Worked examples

These use illustrative project figures.

Worked example 1: UDS against a marked plot on the same land

A 2-acre site (87,120 sq ft) with 40 villas of 2,400 sq ft built-up each (96,000 sq ft in total).

Model Working Land per villa
UDS, by built-up share 87,120 × 2,400 ÷ 96,000 2,178 sq ft (undivided)
Marked plot, if 55% of the land is plots (illustrative) 87,120 × 55% ÷ 40 about 1,198 sq ft (yours alone)

The UDS figure is bigger because it includes your share of roads, parks and the clubhouse land. You can't fence it off. Compare like with like when two projects quote land.

Worked example 2: UDS when villa sizes differ

Same 87,120 sq ft site, but 30 villas of 2,000 sq ft and 10 of 3,600 sq ft (96,000 sq ft total).

Villa type Working UDS per villa
2,000 sq ft 87,120 × 2,000 ÷ 96,000 1,815 sq ft
3,600 sq ft 87,120 × 3,600 ÷ 96,000 3,267 sq ft
Check 30 × 1,815 + 10 × 3,267 87,120 sq ft

If the UDS in your agreement doesn't follow a stated formula, or the shares don't add up to the site, ask why. Some projects split the land by plot area instead of built-up area.

Worked example 3: does this project need RERA?

Project Land Units Registration needed?
A 400 sq m 6 row houses No: under both limits
B 2,000 sq m 8 villas No: eight units or fewer
C 2,000 sq m 12 villas Yes
D 8,094 sq m (2 acres) 40 villas Yes

An unregistered project above both limits is a red flag. A small exempt project still needs every approval above; RERA just won't be policing it.

The DEED test for villa projects (our framework)

Step Question Where to check Red flag
Division Marked plot or undivided share? Draft sale deed, schedule Plot number on the brochure, UDS in the deed
Entitlement Conversion, layout, plan sanction in place? Documents, authority records, RERA uploads "Approval in progress"
Exit OC and khata for each villa? OC, e-Khata or e-Swathu record Possession offered without OC
Dues Which body runs the common areas, and at what cost? Agreement, bye-laws, maintenance schedule No named law for the association

Villa project checklist

  • [ ] Read the draft deed's schedule: plot with boundaries, or UDS?
  • [ ] Get the conversion order and check it on the land records.
  • [ ] Pull an encumbrance certificate for the land (Kaveri steps).
  • [ ] Get the layout approval number and verify it with the authority.
  • [ ] Match the villa type to the sanctioned building plan.
  • [ ] Check RERA registration and uploaded documents.
  • [ ] Confirm carpet area as defined under RERA; see carpet vs super built-up.
  • [ ] Ask who will issue the khata: GBA (e-Khata) or a panchayat (Form 11A and 11B).
  • [ ] Get the water source and the maintenance budget in writing.

Legal and tax note

This is general information, not legal advice. Have a property lawyer read the title, the joint development agreement, the deed schedule and the association structure before you pay an advance; our legal checks guide lists the documents. Ask a CA about tax on the purchase and any later sale.
