Building plan approval in Bengaluru: BDA, BMRDA, GBA or panchayat?

Arvind Patidar Co-founder
Last modified: 19 Oct 2026 • 12 min read
Share:

Short answer: For building plan approval in Bengaluru, first find out where the land sits: a GBA city corporation, a BDA layout, a local planning authority under BMRDA, or a gram panchayat. Then check that the plan sanction, NOCs, occupancy certificate and khata all come from the bodies that cover that land, and that they match each other.

Key takeaways
  • The authority follows the land, not the brochure. "BMRDA approved" isn't a sanction; BMRDA coordinates 12 local planning authorities that do the approving.
  • In a planning area, building needs the planning authority's written permission under Section 14 of the Karnataka Town and Country Planning Act, and unauthorised work can be pulled down.
  • Bigger towers carry more NOCs. At 15 m height, 20 units or 20,000 sq m, new clearances kick in.
  • About 31% of registered projects in our seven-taluk RERA count are in Anekal, Devanahalli and Hosakote, outer taluks where GBA isn't the main approver.
  • Use our where, who, what grid to line up every paper with the right body.

Most approval trouble isn't a forged document. It's a real document from the wrong body, or for a different building. A plan sanctioned for 10 floors doesn't cover 12. A layout approval doesn't sanction the tower on it. This page shows you how to match each paper to the right authority. The full document order is in our legal checks from title to OC.

Who approves what around Bengaluru?

Four kinds of body show up in Bengaluru papers, and each signs different things.

Body What it does What you'll see on paper
GBA and its five city corporations Building plan sanction, commencement and occupancy certificates, khata and property tax inside GBA limits Sanction and OC through the BPAS portal; e-Khata on e-Aasthi
BDA Planning authority for the BDA area; forms and approves layouts Layout approvals; development plan approval for large sites
Other local planning authorities (Anekal, Hoskote, BIAAPA and others) Planning permission in their areas under the KTCP Act Layout approval, commencement certificate
Gram panchayat Records and taxes property in villages Form 9 / Form 11 on e-Swathu

BMRDA itself is the regional body over Bengaluru Urban, Bengaluru Rural and Ramanagara districts. It lists 12 local planning authorities, all working under the Karnataka Town and Country Planning Act 1961 (BMRDA). So when a brochure says "BMRDA approved", ask which of those authorities signed, and for what.

Not sure if a flat is inside GBA? Try our ward and corporation lookup or the answer on city or panchayat. For what changed when GBA replaced BBMP, see what the new city corporations mean for buyers.

What does the law require in a planning area?

Section 14(2) of the KTCP Act says no development or change of land use in a planning area can happen without the planning authority's written permission, given as a commencement certificate. Where agricultural land is involved, it also needs conversion under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act. Section 15(4) lets the authority stop unauthorised work and pull it down (KTCP Act, s.14; s.15).

For you, that means two things. A building without the right permission can face action even after you've bought. And a converted-land order is a separate paper you should see, not something the sanction covers.

How do you check a GBA building plan sanction?

Inside GBA, building permission is applied for online through empanelled professionals, using a common application form, with the external NOCs routed automatically (GBA BPAS FAQ). For a buyer:

  1. Ask for the sanction letter and the sanctioned drawings, with the sanction number and date.
  2. Verify the sanction with the corporation through the BPAS records or the zonal office. GBA's public site has building plan and occupancy certificate lists by year (GBA).
  3. Compare the drawings with the building: floors, units per floor, setbacks, basements and parking.
  4. Check the commencement certificate and, for a finished building, the OC. The OC should carry the same plan number. Our OC and CC guide explains why that matters for power and water.

Which NOCs should an apartment project have?

GBA's BPAS FAQ lists NOCs by building size (GBA BPAS FAQ):

Trigger NOCs listed
Height 15 m and above HAL, Jakkur Flying School, Indian Air Force Yelahanka, Airports Authority of India, Fire Force, BESCOM
Built-up area over 2,000 sq m, or 20+ dwelling units Karnataka State Pollution Control Board, BWSSB
Built-up area over 20,000 sq m State Environment Impact Assessment Authority
Site area over 20,000 sq m Development plan approved by BDA
Site next to a proposed metro line BMRCL

You won't need to collect these yourself, but you should see them. Ask the builder for copies, and check the K-RERA project page, where many are uploaded. Our RERA project search guide shows where.

Worked example 1: which NOCs a tower needs

Illustrative. A project with two towers of 14 floors at about 3 m per floor, 120 flats, averaging 140 sq m of built-up area per flat.

Check Maths Result
Height 14 × 3 m = 42 m Over 15 m: aviation, fire and BESCOM NOCs
Units 120 Over 20: KSPCB and BWSSB
Built-up area 120 × 140 sq m = 16,800 sq m Under 20,000 sq m: SEIAA not triggered by this line

Now make it 160 flats. 160 × 140 = 22,400 sq m, which crosses 20,000 sq m, so you'd expect an environmental clearance too. Real built-up area includes common areas, so the true figure is higher than this rough count. If the numbers suggest a clearance and the builder can't show it, ask why.

Worked example 2: counting units against the plan

Illustrative. The sanctioned drawings show ground plus 12 floors with 8 flats a floor on 12 residential floors: 12 × 8 = 96 flats. The RERA page lists 104 flats.

Source Flats
Sanctioned plan 96
RERA registration 104
Gap 8

Eight flats aren't on the sanctioned plan. There may be a revised sanction, so ask for it. If there isn't one, those flats, and possibly the OC for the building, are at risk. This is the single most useful check you can do with a calculator and two PDFs.

What changes in a panchayat area?

Outside city limits, property is recorded by the gram panchayat on e-Swathu as Form 9 or Form 11. Form 9 is issued only for land converted under the Land Revenue Act with an approved plan, government housing beneficiaries, or Tahsildar-certified gramathana property. Forms 9 and 11 are mandatory for registering non-agricultural panchayat property (e-Swathu FAQ).

So for a panchayat apartment, look for three things: the conversion order, the planning permission from the local planning authority if the village falls in one, and Form 9 or 11 for the land or flat. If the only "approval" is a panchayat tax receipt, that isn't a building plan sanction. We haven't verified each gram panchayat's own licensing role, so ask your lawyer what the panchayat itself has issued and under what power.

For layouts and plots specifically, see our plot approval checklist.

Where are projects being registered?

On the K-RERA register we counted 4,529 approved non-commercial projects in seven Bengaluru taluks (15–16 Oct 2026). 1,412 of them (31.2%) are in Anekal, Devanahalli and Hosakote, outer taluks where a local planning authority, not GBA, is usually the approver. A taluk isn't an authority, so treat that as a rough guide, and read the approval itself. Our Hoskote guide covers one of those areas.

Approval checklist

  • [ ] Confirm whether the land is in GBA, a BDA area, another planning area or a panchayat
  • [ ] Conversion order covering the survey numbers
  • [ ] Layout approval, if the building sits in a layout
  • [ ] Building plan sanction letter and drawings, with number and date
  • [ ] Sanction verified with the issuing body
  • [ ] Commencement certificate
  • [ ] NOCs matching height, units and area
  • [ ] Flat count on the plan equals the count on RERA
  • [ ] OC with the same plan number, for a ready building
  • [ ] e-Khata or Form 9/11 matching the papers
  • [ ] Lake and drain buffers checked (buffer guide)
