Is it safe to buy near a lake or rajakaluve in Bengaluru?

a&k
acre&key
Acrenkey Realty LLP • 19 Oct 2026 • 12 min read
Share:

Short answer: Buying near a lake in Bengaluru can be fine, but only after three checks on the specific property: its distance from the lake's revenue boundary or the drain, the survey map that proves where that boundary is, and the date the plan was approved. Lake buffers became size-based on 18 Feb 2026, and drain encroachments are still being cleared.

Key takeaways
  • Lake buffers now run from 0 m to 30 m by lake size (Act 19 of 2026), measured from the revenue boundary, so you need the lake's area in acres.
  • Primary, secondary and tertiary drains carry 50 m, 25 m and 15 m buffers under RMP-2015, as cited by the Supreme Court in 2019.
  • Pull the official maps: SSLR's Revenue Maps Online has lake survey maps and rajakaluve survey details by village.
  • Encroachments are still being removed. Of 4,106 drain encroachments identified, 2,395 had been cleared by October 2025.
  • Use the distance, document, date test below on any property near water.

What are the buffer rules near lakes and drains?

Lakes. Karnataka Act 19 of 2026, in force from 18 Feb 2026, replaced the flat 30 m lake buffer with one based on the tank's area, measured from its revenue boundary (Bill as passed):

Tank area Buffer
Up to 0.05 acre 0 m
Above 0.05 to 0.10 acre 1 m
Above 0.10 to 1 acre 3 m
Above 1 to 10 acres 6 m
Above 10 to 25 acres 12 m
Above 25 to 100 acres 24 m
Above 100 acres 30 m

Inside the buffer, construction is barred except approved public utilities. The National Green Tribunal took suo motu notice of the change, according to the Times of India (news), so the rule could change again.

Drains. The Supreme Court's judgment of 5 Mar 2019 referred to the RMP-2015 buffers of 50 m from primary drains (rajakaluves), 25 m from secondary drains and 15 m from tertiary drains (judgment). The judgment describes the measurement two ways in different passages, from the drain's edge and from its middle, which matters near the line (worked example 2).

The distance, document, date test (our framework)

Test Question What answers it Stop sign
Distance How far is the building or plot from the lake's revenue boundary, or from the drain? Approved plan with the buffer line; a surveyor's measurement Inside the buffer for that lake size or drain type
Document Which official map shows where the boundary is? SSLR lake survey map; rajakaluve survey details; village cadastral map No one can show you the boundary on an official map
Date When was the plan approved, and under which buffer rule? Plan approval letter; RERA documents Approved before 18 Feb 2026 but built to a smaller buffer than the 30 m then in force

Pass all three and the water's edge is a view, not a legal risk. Fail one and it goes to your lawyer before any money moves.

Which maps should you pull?

  • Lake survey maps. The Survey Settlement and Land Records department's Revenue Maps Online has a "Lake (Survey)" section listing lake survey maps by district, taluk, hobli, village and survey number, including Bengaluru Urban.
  • Rajakaluve survey details. The same portal has a "Raj Kalve Survey Details" section.
  • Village cadastral maps. Also on Revenue Maps Online, by village.
  • Land records. The RTC for the property's and the lake's survey numbers on Bhoomi tells you the lake's recorded extent.
  • The approved plan. It should show the buffer line, and the building should sit behind it.

Phone apps that overlay survey boundaries are useful for a first look, but a survey map and a licensed surveyor's measurement are what your lawyer will rely on.

Worked example 1: same distance, different answers

Illustrative. Two plots, each with its boundary 15 m from the revenue boundary of a nearby lake.

Plot A Plot B
Lake area (from the RTC) 3 acres 60 acres
Buffer under Act 19 of 2026 6 m 24 m
Plot boundary from the lake 15 m 15 m
Result Outside, with 9 m to spare Inside by 9 m
Under the old 30 m rule Inside by 15 m Inside by 15 m

Plot A became buildable on paper in February 2026. Plot B is still inside the buffer. Neither answer is possible without the lake's area from revenue records, and if the NGT changes the rule, Plot A's position could change too.

Worked example 2: edge or centre?

Illustrative. A building 22 m from the edge of a secondary drain that's 12 m wide (so 28 m from its centre line). Secondary drain buffer: 25 m.

Reading Buffer runs to Building at Result
From the drain's edge 25 m from the edge 22 m from the edge Inside by 3 m
From the drain's centre 25 m from the centre (19 m from the edge) 28 m from the centre Outside by 3 m

When a building sits this close to the line, the reading decides the answer. Ask your lawyer which applies, and check how the approving authority drew the buffer on the plan.

What happens to buildings that encroach?

They can be demolished. Figures the Deputy Chief Minister tabled in the Legislative Assembly, as reported in October 2025, showed 4,106 stormwater-drain encroachments identified, of which 2,395 had been removed, 1,325 were still under survey, 198 were in court and 188 were being cleared, leaving 1,711 uncleared (news report).

Worked example 3: what those numbers tell a buyer

Status Count Share of 4,106
Removed 2,395 58.3%
Still uncleared (survey, court, in progress) 1,711 41.7%

More than four in ten identified encroachments were still standing at that point. A building that has stood for years next to a drain isn't proof it's legal. It may simply not have been reached yet.

What questions should you ask the builder or seller?

  1. What's the lake's survey number and area in acres, and which buffer applies?
  2. Where's the buffer line on the approved plan, and how far is the building behind it?
  3. Was the plan approved before or after 18 Feb 2026, and under which buffer?
  4. Where's the nearest primary or secondary drain, and was its buffer measured from the edge or the centre?
  5. Has the property ever appeared on an encroachment list or received a notice?
  6. Where does the site's stormwater go?
  7. Did water enter the basement or approach road in the last monsoon?

Questions 6 and 7 are about flooding, not law. A property outside every buffer can still sit in a low spot. Visit after heavy rain.

What mistakes do buyers make near lakes and drains?

  • Quoting the old 30 m lake rule. It's size-based now.
  • Not getting the lake's area. Without it, the buffer can't be worked out.
  • Trusting a phone app over a survey map. Apps are a first look only.
  • Assuming an old building is legal. Encroachment clearance is still under way.
  • Ignoring the approval date. A plan approved before February 2026 was meant to keep 30 m.
  • Checking the law but not the ground. Legal distance doesn't keep a basement dry.

What should you do if...

...the property is inside a buffer? Treat it as a Stop. Our legal checks guide explains why it's not something a seller can fix.

...it's just outside the new lake buffer but inside the old 30 m? Ask your lawyer about the risk while the NGT matter is pending, and check the approval date.

...no one can show you the boundary on a map? Pay for a licensed surveyor before you pay a token.

...the building flooded last monsoon? Ask what was done about it, and check your insurance. Our answer on home insurance for a new flat covers what to ask.
