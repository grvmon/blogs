Site visit checklist: inside the flat and around the building

a&k
acre&key
Acrenkey Realty LLP • 19 Oct 2026 • 7 min read
Share:

Short answer: Use this site visit checklist before you book, not at handover. Visit twice: once at your commute's peak hour and once in the evening. Inside, check the actual unit's light, layout and finishes. Outside, check the approach road, drainage, water source and common areas. Then ask residents what they pay for water and maintenance.

Key takeaways
  • Two visits beat one long one: peak-hour traffic and evening noise don't show on a Sunday morning.
  • See your unit, not the sample flat. Sample flats often have upgraded fittings and furniture chosen to fit.
  • Water is the question to ask residents: source, supply hours and tanker spend.
  • Some roads flood with very little rain. A 2023 KSNDMC report put Panathur Main Road at 1 cm (news).
  • Our TWICE check: traffic, water, inside, common areas, evening.

A site visit is when a brochure meets the street. This checklist is for the visit before you pay a token in Bengaluru. For the handover inspection, use our possession checks instead.

T: How do you get there and back?

  1. Drive or ride your real commute at peak hour, both ways.
  2. Is the approach road wide enough for two cars and a delivery truck?
  3. Is there a footpath, and where's the nearest bus stop or metro station?
  4. What's on the way: a level crossing, a narrow bridge, a market that blocks traffic?

W: Where does the water come from and go?

  1. Ask residents nearby about the water source, supply hours and tankers.
  2. Look at drains along the road. Are they open, silted or blocked?
  3. Check the road against our KSNDMC flood map and the project area against our flood exposure index.
  4. Is there a lake, drain or low-lying plot next to the site?

I: What's the unit really like?

  1. Ask to see your actual unit, or one on a similar floor with the same facing.
  2. Which rooms get direct sun, and when? See our floor and corner guide.
  3. Measure two rooms and compare them with the plan's carpet area (carpet area explainer).
  4. Note what the sample flat has that the specification list doesn't.
  5. Check the mobile signal in every room.

C: What do the common areas tell you?

  1. For a ready building: lifts, stairwells, the basement and the generator. Upkeep now predicts your maintenance bills.
  2. For an under-construction project: does the work on site match the stage in the latest RERA update? Our project review method shows where to look.
  3. Where's the parking, and how tight are the ramps and turns?
  4. Are there power lines, a mobile tower, or a garbage point next to the site?

E: What's it like in the evening?

  1. Come back after dark. Is the street lit? Is it noisy?
  2. Where do people park on the road at night?
  3. Ask a resident: "If you were buying here again, would you?"

Worked examples

Worked example 1: what a longer commute costs

Illustrative. Your peak-hour drive from Flat B takes 25 minutes more each way than from Flat A, on 22 working days a month.

Item Working Result
Extra time a month 25 × 2 × 22 1,100 minutes, about 18 hours
Extra time a year 18.3 hours × 12 about 220 hours
Extra distance (assumed 8 km each way) 16 km × 22 352 km a month

Time is the bigger cost. About 27 eight-hour working days a year in traffic is a fair thing to weigh against a lower price. Put it in the surroundings score in our side-by-side comparison.

Worked example 2: putting a number on tanker water

Illustrative, using assumed figures. Replace them with what residents tell you. A 200-flat society buys four tankers a day in summer at ₹1,000 each.

Item Working Result
Society spend a day 4 × ₹1,000 ₹4,000
A month (30 days) ₹4,000 × 30 ₹1,20,000
Per flat a month ₹1,20,000 ÷ 200 ₹600
Per flat over four summer months ₹600 × 4 ₹2,400

The money is modest. The bigger question is what happens if tankers are short. Ask how many days last summer the society ran out.

The TWICE check (our framework)

Letter Covers When to go
T: Traffic Commute, approach road, transport Weekday peak hour
W: Water Source, drains, flood points After rain, if you can
I: Inside Your unit, sun, size, signal Daytime
C: Common areas Upkeep, construction stage, parking Daytime
E: Evening Lights, noise, parking, residents After dark

Score each line 1 to 5 after each visit. Anything at 2 or below goes on your list of questions for the seller.
