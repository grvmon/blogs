# Devanahalli property rates (2026): metro, water and flood risk

**Short answer:** Official Devanahalli property rates (guidance values) for town sites run ₹4,500 to ₹44,000 per sq m, or about ₹418 to ₹4,088 per sq ft. No major consultant publishes a Devanahalli price series. The airport metro is targeted for 2027 but won't reach the town itself, and the town's water still depends heavily on groundwater.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **Outside the city:** Devanahalli town is run by a Town Municipal Council, and planning in the airport area sits with BIAAPA, not BDA or GBA.
> - **Prices are a gap:** Knight Frank's table doesn't cover Devanahalli, and listing-portal prices aren't evidence. Registered values on encumbrance certificates are the most useful guide.
> - **Metro:** the Blue Line's Hebbal–airport stretch is targeted for September 2027 (news). It serves the airport, not Devanahalli town.
> - **Water:** a shallow-aquifer plant meets about 10% of the town's demand; a ₹44.87 crore AMRUT 2.0 water scheme is at the project-report stage.
> - **Our 4A check:** authority, approval, aquifer, access.

Devanahalli is the old fort town next to Kempegowda International Airport, about where the city's northern growth meets farmland. Many people look here because of the airport and the aerospace and IT parks planned around it. ## Who governs Devanahalli?

Three bodies matter, and none is the Greater Bengaluru Authority:

1. **Devanahalli Town Municipal Council** runs the town, its property records and water supply ([Bengaluru Rural district site](https://bangalorerural.nic.in/en/public-utility/devanahalli-tmc/)).
2. **Gram panchayats** run villages outside the town. Their property records sit on e-Swathu.
3. **BIAAPA**, the Bengaluru International Airport Area Planning Authority, is the local planning authority for the Devanahalli area, one of the authorities listed by BMRDA under the KTCP Act ([BMRDA](https://bmrda.karnataka.gov.in/8/local-planning-areas/en)).

This affects what you pay and what you check. The stamp duty surcharge is 2% of duty in municipal areas and 3% in panchayat areas. A layout should have land conversion, BIAAPA approval and released sites. Our [plot buyer's guide](https://acrenkey.com/blogs/buying-a-plot-in-bengaluru-bda-bmrda-dtcp-approved-layout-checks/) and [approvals guide](https://acrenkey.com/blogs/bda-bmrda-gba-or-panchayat-checking-approvals-before-you-buy/) walk through these checks.

### Market prices

This is the honest gap. Knight Frank's Bengaluru price table doesn't include Devanahalli, and ANAROCK's market tables cover other areas. Listing portals publish asking prices, which aren't evidence of what anything sold for. **We have no official or consultant market series for Devanahalli to cite.** The most useful evidence is registered values for the same layout or building, from encumbrance certificates on Kaveri.

What we can say: Knight Frank calls North Bengaluru the city's fastest-growing residential corridor, with launches up 10% and sales up 11% in H1 2026.

### What's being built

Devanahalli taluk wasn't among the three east taluks in our RERA study, so we don't have a delivery figure. Check each project's completion date and extensions on [Karnataka RERA](https://rera.karnataka.gov.in/viewAllProjects?language=en). Plotted layouts must be RERA-registered too, above the size threshold.

## Is there a metro to Devanahalli?

Not to the town. The Blue Line runs from Central Silk Board via KR Puram and Hebbal to the airport, with stations at Airport City and the terminals (station names per news reports). The Hebbal–airport section is targeted for September 2027, and the full line for March 2028, according to the Bengaluru development minister ([Times of India, 9 Oct 2026](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/bengaluru/bengaluru-metro-blue-line-delayed-to-march-2028-how-the-58km-airport-corridor-will-open-in-phases-which-stretches-will-be-ready-first-and-when-commuters-can-expect-services-from-central-silk-board-to-kia/photostory/134829302.cms), news). Deccan Herald reported in August 2026 that work beyond Yelahanka was lagging, so treat these as targets.

From the town, you'd still need a road trip to the nearest airport-line station. Time it before you count it.

## Where does Devanahalli get its water?

Devanahalli isn't part of Bengaluru's Cauvery network. The town council has relied largely on groundwater. Since August 2024, a shallow-aquifer treatment plant near Sihineerukere has supplied about 6.4 lakh litres a day, roughly 10% of the town's demand. The lake is recharged partly with treated wastewater from the Hebbal–Nagawara valley ([The Hindu, Oct 2024](https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/bangalore/treatment-plant-uses-aquifer-to-supply-6-lakh-litres-of-potable-water-to-devanahalli-town-near-bengaluru/article68717754.ece), news).

A ₹44.87 crore water supply scheme for the town is listed under AMRUT 2.0 at "DPR prepared" stage ([AMRUT 2.0 platform](https://amrut.mohua.gov.in/approvedProjects/list/OXhRK3hRdEZJSC9hdmxYZXVTM0ZFdz09/UXg5RVludk5MdEhqNzQybml3QVcrQT09)). That isn't a completion date. In villages and new layouts, expect borewells and tankers unless a project proves otherwise. Our [water questions](https://acrenkey.com/blogs/water-bengaluru-bwssb-borewell-tanker-questions-to-ask/) list what to ask.

## Is Devanahalli flood-prone?

There's no public flood-point list for Devanahalli; the city's list covers only the five Bengaluru corporations. The risk to check is site-level: tank beds, valley drains and low plots in new layouts. Lake buffers became size-based (0 to 30 m) under Act 19 of 2026, measured from the lake's revenue boundary. Our [lake and drain checks](https://acrenkey.com/blogs/flood-risk-lake-buffers-and-rajakaluve-checks-before-you-buy/) show how to pull the survey maps, and a visit after heavy rain tells you the rest.

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: town or village, same plot price

Illustrative. A plot bought at ₹40,00,000.

| Charge | Devanahalli town (municipal) | Panchayat village |
|---|---|---|
| Stamp duty 5% | ₹2,00,000 | ₹2,00,000 |
| Cess 10% of duty | ₹20,000 | ₹20,000 |
| Surcharge (2% / 3% of duty) | ₹4,000 | ₹6,000 |
| Registration 2% | ₹80,000 | ₹80,000 |
| **Total** | **₹3,04,000** | **₹3,06,000** |

Only ₹2,000 apart. The real differences are records, approvals and water. Our [full cost guide](https://acrenkey.com/blogs/full-cost-bengaluru-flat-stamp-duty-registration-gst/) covers the rest.

### Worked example 2: the same site size on two town lines

Illustrative. A 1,200 sq ft site (111.48 sq m).

| Gazette line | Guidance value | Minimum charges at 7.6% |
|---|---|---|
| B B Road, ₹44,000 per sq m | ₹49,05,285 | ₹3,72,802 |
| Shivanna Layout, ₹18,000 per sq m | ₹20,06,708 | ₹1,52,510 |

The floor on B B Road is more than double. If you're paying close to the guidance value, find the exact line first.

### Worked example 3: a plot priced below the floor

Illustrative. A 1,200 sq ft site on Taluk Office Road, guidance value ₹27,87,094.

| Agreed price | Guidance value ÷ price | Above 110%? |
|---|---|---|
| ₹25,00,000 | 1.115 | Yes |
| ₹26,00,000 | 1.072 | No |

At ₹25 lakh, the guidance value is more than 110% of the price, which can trigger the income-tax gap rules for both seller and buyer, as explained in our [guidance value vs market price post](https://acrenkey.com/insights/guidance-value-vs-market-price-east-bengaluru/). Duty would also be charged on ₹27.87 lakh (₹2,11,819 at 7.6%). Check with your CA.

## The 4A Devanahalli check (our framework)

| Check | Question | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority** | Town council or gram panchayat? | Tax receipt; e-Aasthi (town) or e-Swathu (village) record |
| **Approval** | Converted land, BIAAPA-approved layout or plan, sites released? | Conversion order, approved plan, release letter, RERA page |
| **Aquifer** | Where does water come from, and is it enough? | Written source from the seller or builder; borewell details; town connection |
| **Access** | How long to the airport, the city and the nearest metro station? | Drive it at peak hour; Blue Line status |

## Devanahalli buyer's checklist

- [ ] Town council or panchayat confirmed from the tax receipt
- [ ] Property record (e-Aasthi or e-Swathu Form 9/11) matches the deed
- [ ] Land conversion order and BIAAPA layout or building approval seen
- [ ] Site released by the planning authority, if it's in a layout
- [ ] Guidance value line found on Kaveri for the exact property
- [ ] Past registered values checked on encumbrance certificates
- [ ] Water source confirmed in writing
- [ ] Tank beds and drains checked on survey maps; site seen after heavy rain
- [ ] RERA registration checked for projects and larger layouts
- [ ] Title checked by a lawyer ([our legal checks guide](https://acrenkey.com/blogs/bengaluru-property-legal-checks-from-title-to-oc/))

## What mistakes do buyers make in Devanahalli?

- **Treating "near the airport" as a price.** There's no published market series; use registered values.
- **Counting the airport metro as a town metro.** It serves the airport.
- **Skipping the approval chain.** Conversion, BIAAPA approval and release are separate papers.
- **Assuming piped water.** The town's own scheme is still at the project-report stage.

*This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Ask a property lawyer to check title, conversion and approvals, and a CA about the tax side.*

## Frequently asked questions

### What are property rates in Devanahalli in 2026?

Official guidance values for Devanahalli town sites run ₹4,500 to ₹44,000 per sq m, about ₹418 to ₹4,088 per sq ft, under the 2023 gazette. No major consultant publishes a Devanahalli market series, so check registered values in the same layout on Kaveri encumbrance certificates.

### Is Devanahalli good to live in?

It suits people who work at or near the airport, or who want land at lower guidance values than the city. Against that, there's no metro in the town, water depends heavily on groundwater, and approvals need careful checking. Run the authority, approval, aquifer, access check.

### Is there a metro to Devanahalli?

Not to the town. The Blue Line's Hebbal–airport stretch, with stations at Airport City and the terminals (station names per news reports), is targeted for September 2027, according to October 2026 news reports. From Devanahalli town you'd still need to travel by road to the nearest station.

### Who approves layouts in Devanahalli?

BIAAPA, the Bengaluru International Airport Area Planning Authority, is the local planning authority listed by BMRDA for the area. A layout also needs a land conversion order, and sites should be released before sale. The town council or panchayat then issues the property record.

### Does Devanahalli get Cauvery water?

No. Devanahalli isn't on Bengaluru's Cauvery network. The town relies largely on groundwater, with a shallow-aquifer plant meeting about 10% of demand since 2024. A ₹44.87 crore AMRUT 2.0 water scheme is at the project-report stage. Ask every seller for the water source in writing.

### Is Devanahalli flood-prone?

There's no public flood-point list for Devanahalli. Risk is site-level: tank beds, valley drains and low plots in new layouts. Check the lake's revenue boundary and buffer on survey maps, and visit the site after heavy rain before you buy.

### What is the guidance value in Devanahalli?

Under the 2023 gazette, Devanahalli town lines include ₹44,000 per sq m on B B Road, ₹30,000 on Bajar Road, ₹25,000 on Taluk Office Road and ₹18,000 in Shivanna Layout. Villages and named layouts have their own lines on Kaveri.

### How much stamp duty will I pay in Devanahalli?

About 7.6% of the price in the town (municipal area) and 7.65% in panchayat villages: 5% duty, 10% cess, the 2% or 3% surcharge on duty, and 2% registration. On a ₹40 lakh plot that's ₹3,04,000 or ₹3,06,000.
