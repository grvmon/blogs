Corner flat and floor choice: 12 questions before you pick

G
By Gaurav • 19 Oct 2026 • 7 min read
Share:

Short answer: On a corner flat and floor choice, there's no single right answer. Ask what the floor rise and corner charges add after GST and stamp duty, which walls take the afternoon sun, what you share walls with, how many lifts run on backup, and whether the extra is worth it to you. We haven't found resale data on these charges.

Key takeaways
  • Extra charges grow on the way through. A floor rise charge also attracts GST (if under construction) and stamp duty.
  • Financed, a ₹5 lakh charge costs about ₹4,339 a month for 20 years at 8.5%.
  • Corner means more outside wall: more light and air, and more sun and rain on it.
  • High floors depend on lifts and pumps. Buildings 21 m or above need a fire NOC in Karnataka.
  • Our LIGHT check: lifts, insulation from sun, gaps and shared walls, height and safety, total cost.

Once you've chosen a project, the unit is the last big choice, and it often carries its own charges. These 12 questions help you decide what you're paying for. If you're still choosing between projects, start with our side-by-side comparison.

L: Can you get up and down easily?

  1. How many lifts serve your tower, and do they run on power backup? On a high floor, a lift breakdown is a daily problem.
  2. What's the water pressure on your floor? Run the taps on a similar floor during your visit.

I: How much sun and heat will the flat take?

  1. Which walls face west or south-west? Those take the afternoon sun. Visit at around 4 pm to feel it.
  2. Is this the top floor? Ask who maintains the roof waterproofing above you, and how heat is handled.

G: What's on the other side of your walls?

  1. Does the flat have windows on two sides for cross-ventilation? This is the main reason to want a corner.
  2. Do bedrooms share a wall with the lift, stairs or a service shaft? Noise travels.
  3. What does the view face, and can it be built over? An open plot next door may not stay open.

H: Is the height safe and practical?

  1. Is the building 21 metres or more? Since March 2023, high-rises of 21 m or above need a fire NOC from the Karnataka State Fire and Emergency Services (our OC guide). Ask to see it.
  2. On a ground or lower floor, how does the site drain? Check the flood exposure index and our KSNDMC flood map.
  3. Is privacy an issue at ground level? Look at what faces your windows and balcony.

T: What does it cost in total?

  1. What's the floor rise charge, from which floor, and on which area? Also ask about any corner or preferred location charge.
  2. Will you get it back? We haven't found published data on how much of these charges buyers recover at resale. Treat them as money you spend on comfort, not as an investment.

Worked examples

Worked example 1: what a floor rise charge really costs

Illustrative figures. Floor rise of ₹50 a sq ft for each floor above the 4th, on 1,250 sq ft super built-up, for a 14th-floor flat in an under-construction project. Ask for your project's actual rate.

Item Working Amount
Floor rise 10 floors × ₹50 × 1,250 sq ft ₹6,25,000
GST at 5% 5% × ₹6,25,000 ₹31,250
Stamp duty and registration 7.6% × ₹6,25,000 ₹47,500
Total ₹7,03,750

The quoted charge is ₹6.25 lakh. What you actually pay is about ₹7 lakh. Our full cost guide covers how GST and duty are worked out.

Worked example 2: a charge on your EMI

Say ₹5,00,000 of a floor or corner charge goes on your loan at 8.5% for 20 years.

Item Working Amount
EMI per lakh 8.5%, 20 years ₹867.82
Extra EMI 5 × ₹867.82 ₹4,339 a month
Paid over 20 years ₹4,339 × 240 ₹10,41,360

That's ₹10.4 lakh for a ₹5 lakh charge. Check it against your budget with our EMI calculator and EMI-to-income check.

Corner flat vs middle flat

Corner flat Middle flat
Light and air Windows on more sides, if the plan uses them Depends on the plan
Heat and rain More outside wall exposed Less outside wall
Shared walls Fewer More
Cost Often carries a location charge Usually the base rate
Layout Can have odd shapes or longer corridors Often simpler

A well-planned middle flat can beat a poorly oriented corner. Check the actual plan, not the label.
