15 questions before buying near a metro station

Nitin Gupta
Investments · Decision Framework • Published: 19 Oct 2026 • 8 min read
Share:

Short answer: Before buying near a metro station in Bengaluru, check the line's real stage (running, under construction or only proposed), walk the route to the station gate, and listen for noise from the viaduct. Ask how long construction will block your road, and what markup you're paying. Dates slip: the Blue Line's full opening is now reported for March 2028.

Key takeaways
  • Only three lines run: Purple, Green and Yellow, about 96 km (news, October 2026).
  • Dates move: the airport line's Hebbal stretch is targeted for September 2027 and the full Blue Line for March 2028 (news).
  • Distance on a map isn't the walk. Ten minutes on foot is roughly 800 m at an easy pace, if there's a footpath.
  • A markup has a monthly cost: ₹8 lakh financed is about ₹6,943 a month at 8.5% over 20 years.
  • Our TRAIN check: timeline, route to the gate, annoyances, investment markup, next buyer.

"Five minutes from the metro" is on half the hoardings in Bengaluru. Sometimes the metro exists. Sometimes it's a line on a plan. These 15 questions help you work out what you're getting.

T: What's the timeline?

  1. Is the line running, under construction, approved or only proposed? Our metro lines status puts each line on a ladder.
  2. What's the latest reported opening date, and how far has it already slipped? Add a buffer. Our North Bengaluru guide shows how this plays out along the airport line.
  3. For Phase 3 and beyond, is the station location final? See our Phase 3 routes guide.

R: What's the route to the gate?

  1. How long is the actual walk from your building's gate to the station entrance, not the line on a map?
  2. Is there a footpath, a safe crossing, and lighting at night?
  3. Which side of the road is the entrance on? Crossing a highway can add ten minutes.

A: What will annoy you?

  1. How close is the viaduct or station to your windows? Elevated lines bring noise and vibration. Visit when trains are running, or near a running stretch of the same line. Our floor and corner guide covers which side to choose.
  2. How many more years of construction will narrow your road, and how crowded will the station area get with autos and parking?
  3. Is any part of the site in the metro's land acquisition area? Ask your lawyer to check the encumbrance certificate and records for acquisition notices.

I: What markup are you paying?

  1. How much more is this flat than a similar one 2 km away? Ask for both prices.
  2. Do guidance values show a metro effect here? Our metro uplift study found main-road frontage explains much of the gap.
  3. What does the markup cost you each month until the line opens?

N: Who's the next buyer?

  1. Will a buyer in five years pay for this location? That depends on the line opening and working well.
  2. Is the station on a line that goes where people work?
  3. Will future construction nearby block your light or view? Station areas often attract taller buildings.

Worked examples

Worked example 1: does the commute pay for the markup?

Illustrative, with assumed figures. A flat near a station costs ₹8,00,000 more than a similar one 2 km away. You finance the difference at 8.5% over 20 years. You'd swap a ₹400 daily cab for a ₹60 metro trip, 22 days a month.

Item Working Amount
Extra EMI 8 × ₹867.82 ₹6,943 a month
Commute saving (₹400 − ₹60) × 22 ₹7,480 a month
Net About ₹537 a month ahead

It roughly breaks even, but only once the line is running and only if you'd use it every working day. Replace the assumptions with your own numbers with our EMI calculator.

Worked example 2: paying for a line that isn't open yet

Same ₹8,00,000 markup, paid in October 2026 for a stretch reported to open in March 2028, 17 months away.

Item Working Amount
Interest on the markup while you wait ₹8,00,000 × 8.5% × 17 ÷ 12 ₹96,333
If it slips another 12 months ₹8,00,000 × 8.5% × 12 ÷ 12 +₹68,000

Waiting costs real money, and you get none of the benefit until trains run. A markup near a running station is easier to justify than one near a planned line.

  • [ ] Noise checked at a running stretch

  • [ ] Price of a similar flat 2 km away noted
  • [ ] Markup cost worked out to the opening date, plus a year

Lawyer note

Land acquisition along a metro alignment is a title question. Have a property lawyer check the records for the site before you pay.
