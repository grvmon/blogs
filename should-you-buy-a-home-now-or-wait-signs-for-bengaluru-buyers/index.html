Should you buy a home now or wait? Signs for Bengaluru buyers

A
By Arvind • 19 Oct 2026 • 9 min read
Share:

Short answer: Whether to buy a home now or wait in Bengaluru depends more on you than the market. In October 2026 the RBI raised repo to 5.50% and signalled no near-term cuts, prices rose 9% in a year, and unsold stock is growing. If you're financially ready and staying five years or more, waiting for a dip is a gamble.

Key takeaways
  • Rates turned: the RBI raised repo by 25 bps on 7 Oct 2026 and said rate cuts are off the table in the near term.
  • Prices are still rising: Knight Frank's Bengaluru average was ₹9,354 per sq ft in H1 2026, up 9% in a year.
  • Stock is building: ANAROCK counted 72,800 unsold units in Q1 2026, up 24% in a year.
  • A rate cut is easily cancelled: a 3.75% price rise wipes out the EMI saving from a 0.5-point cut.
  • Our READY test tells you whether you're ready, whatever the market does.

Nobody can time a property market reliably, including us. What you can do is read the current signals honestly and check whether your own situation says buy or wait.

What are the market signals saying in October 2026?

Signal Latest reading Leans towards
Repo rate Raised to 5.50% on 7 Oct 2026; "rate cuts are off the table in the near term" (RBI) Waiting won't bring cheaper loans soon
Prices ₹9,354 per sq ft in H1 2026, up 9% in a year (Knight Frank) Rising, not falling
Unsold stock 72,800 units in Q1 2026, up 12% in the quarter and 24% in a year (ANAROCK) More choice and room to negotiate
Sales 16,450 units in Q1 2026, down 5% in the quarter, up 10% in a year (ANAROCK) Demand steady
New project approvals 309 in January to September 2026, against 387 a year earlier, across seven taluks (our K-RERA count) Fewer new launches ahead
Affordability EMI-to-income 35% in H1 2026 (our affordability study) Stretched but under 50%

The signals point both ways. Rising stock helps buyers negotiate. Rising prices and a rate rise make waiting more expensive. At Q1 2026's sales pace, 72,800 units is about 13 months of stock.

Is waiting for a rate cut worth it?

Probably not in the near term, given the RBI's October stance. And even when cuts come, a small price rise cancels them.

Worked examples

These use a ₹90 lakh flat, an 80% loan over 20 years, and the approved 8.5% illustrative rate.

Worked example 1: what a year's wait costs at different price growth

Price growth in the year Price after a year Extra price Extra stamp charges at 7.6% Total extra
0% ₹90,00,000 Nil Nil Nil
5% ₹94,50,000 ₹4,50,000 ₹34,200 ₹4,84,200
9% (Knight Frank's last year) ₹98,10,000 ₹8,10,000 ₹61,560 ₹8,71,560

Waiting only pays if prices fall, or if you'd otherwise buy the wrong home in a hurry. You'd be paying rent either way.

Worked example 2: a rate cut vs a price rise

Buy now at 8.5% Wait for 8.0%
Loan ₹72,00,000 ₹72,00,000
EMI ₹62,483 ₹60,224
Monthly saving ₹2,260

Now let the price rise while you wait. At 8.0%, ₹62,483 a month services a loan of about ₹74,70,000, which is 3.75% more. So if prices rise more than 3.75% before the cut arrives, waiting leaves you worse off. Prices rose 9% last year.

When does waiting make sense?

  • You haven't saved the down payment plus about 7.6% for charges.
  • Your job or city may change within three years.
  • You haven't chosen an area, or haven't seen it in the monsoon (see our flood-prone areas guide).
  • The project you like has no RERA registration or approvals yet. Our K-RERA step-by-step shows how to check.
  • You'd have to stretch past half your income for the EMI.

The READY test (our framework)

Letter Question Ready if
R: Reserves Do you have the down payment, charges and six months of expenses? Yes, without borrowing from family at short notice
E: EMI headroom Can you pay the EMI at your rate plus 2%? Yes, under about half of take-home pay
A: Area Have you settled on two or three areas and checked water and flood? Yes
D: Duration Will you stay at least five to seven years? Yes, so costs are spread out
Y: Your job Is your income stable for the next few years? Yes

Five yeses: market timing matters less than finding the right home. Two or more noes: waiting is sensible, whatever prices do.

Buy-or-wait checklist

Will a guidance value revision change the timing?

Possibly, for a few buyers. A revision of 12% to 15% was reported as being prepared in February 2026 (The New Indian Express, news). Our position: the 2023 rates are still in force; check Kaveri. Stamp duty is charged on the higher of price and guidance value, so a revision only matters where the new guidance value would exceed your price. Our stamp duty calculator shows the difference.
